The decision is made on a left press: `onButton` (L1624) → `beginDrag` (L1889) → `beginGridDrag` (L2032), which tries these cases in order and stops at the first match:

1. **Shift held** (L2046): toggles whether the note is selected, or starts a marquee. No resize or move.
2. **Pointer inside the selection box** (L2061): if `selectionBounds` (L1456) finds two or more selected notes and their box contains the pointer, the press moves the group. Edges are never tested here, so a note inside a group can't be resized.
3. **Edge grab** (L2077): `edgeGrabAt(position, grab)` (L1422). If it hits, the drag becomes `DRAG_RESIZE`, and `drag_left_edge = grab.left_edge` picks the left or right edge. The selection is left unchanged.
4. **Note body** (L2093): `noteIndexAt(step, pitch)` (L1406) uses the half-open span `start <= step < end`. A hit selects only that note and arms `beginMove`.
5. Otherwise, empty space clears the selection or paints a new note (L2102, L2110).

`onHover` (L1590–1601) checks the same box → edge → body order to pick the cursor.

**How the grab zone is computed**, in `edgeGrabAt` L1437–1439: `zone = min(EDGE_GRAB_W, max(1, (x1-x0)/3))`. `EDGE_GRAB_W` is 4 px (`PianoRollGeometry.hpp:127`). So notes 12 px or wider get ±4 px, narrower notes get a third of their width, and the floor is 1 px. The code comment says this scaling keeps a fixed zone from covering a narrow note entirely, which would make it impossible to move. Notes are scanned from the last one drawn to the first (topmost first), on the active track and the pointer's pitch row. **The right edge is tested first** (L1441), so it wins if the two zones overlap.

**Why a short note's left edge can't be grabbed:** L1445 also requires `(x1-x0) > EDGE_GRAB_MIN_NOTE_W`, which is 4 × 2.5 = 10 px (`PianoRollGeometry.hpp:128`). The comment says this is so a left-edge grab "cannot be confused with grabbing the right one". On a note 10 px wide or less, a press near the left edge drops through to case 4 and moves the note. Only the right edge stays resizable.
