Click builds the one-line short help shown in command listings from the first paragraph of a command's help text, in `_make_default_short_help` in `src/click/utils.py`. Two things are wrong with how it abbreviates.

1. It treats every word that ends with a period as the end of the sentence, so help text like `Weigh apples vs. pears.` is cut down to `Weigh apples vs.`. A period should end the sentence only when it ends the text, or when the next word does not start with a lowercase letter. So `Weigh apples vs. pears.` stays whole, `Pick a fruit. 3 remain.` becomes `Pick a fruit.`, and `123 567 9. aaaa bbb` continues past `9.` and is then shortened by length.

2. Shortening to `max_length` is unreliable. The rules should be: if the sentence-trimmed text fits within `max_length`, return it unchanged. Otherwise keep as many whole leading words as fit together with the `...` suffix inside `max_length`, never splitting a word (hyphenated words count as one word), and return them followed by `...`. If not even one word fits, or `max_length` is smaller than the suffix itself (including zero or negative values), return just `...`. Examples with `max_length` 10: `123 567 90123.` gives `123 567...`; `123 5678 xxxxxx` gives `123...`; `Weigh apples vs. pears and plums.` with 20 gives `Weigh apples vs....`; `well-behaved.` with 8 gives `...`; `supercalifragilistic` with 10 gives `...`; `ab cd` with 3 or with 2 gives `...`; `ab` with 2 gives `ab`.

Keep the existing behavior otherwise: only the first paragraph is considered, runs of whitespace collapse to single spaces, and a leading `\b` no-rewrap marker is ignored. Fix the function and add tests for the new cases.
